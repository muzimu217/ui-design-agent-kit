# Direction Contract

Assigned world: kiln shelf beneath glazed cups, adapted to a configurable smartphone.

Audience identification: strong because tactile finish names, batch marks, and shelf staging distinguish the page from generic phone ecommerce.

Product clarity: strong because phone render, finish/capacity controls, live price, and bag action remain literal and adjacent.

Challenger verdicts:

- Teletext magazine: declined. Its block grid would make product imagery and premium material less inspectable. Kept: direct address-like clarity in small batch labels.
- Depot blind courses: competitive on state change, weaker on shopper recognition. Kept: one-step, whole-row state transitions for summary updates.
- Cloud quarry: declined. Its transient architecture conflicts with a concrete shopping decision. Kept: blue void contrast between sections.
- Kiln glaze shelf: assigned and built. Owns the page world.
- Shareware installer: declined. Its linear wizard would over-constrain side-by-side comparison. Kept: explicit step/state feedback for add-to-bag.
- Cassette futurist deck: competitive on tactile controls, weaker on product clarity. Kept: labeled controls with physical-feeling press feedback.

Raised direction: the shareware donor adds explicit success/recovery copy; depot blind adds stepped whole-row summary continuity; cassette deck adds disciplined labels and control zoning.
